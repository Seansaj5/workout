The work

    Face and neck, every day

    Rest days too. Takes about eight minutes. Do it in the mirror so you can check you're not straining your jaw.

    1. Chin tucks3 × 10Sit tall, slide the head straight back, hold 5 seconds. Builds the deep neck flexors and fixes forward head posture, which does more for your jawline than any facial move.
    2. Neck isometrics3 × 20sPalm against forehead, then back of head, then each side. Push at about 70 percent, no movement.
    3. Tongue to palate2 minWhole tongue flat against the roof of the mouth, lips sealed, breathing through the nose. Then just try to hold it as your default all day.
    4. Jaw-opening resistance2 × 10Fist under the chin, open the mouth slowly against light pressure. Gentle. Stop the second anything clicks or aches.
    5. Mastic or hard gum10 min capTrains the masseter, which is the muscle that actually widens the lower face. Ten to fifteen minutes a day is the ceiling. Chewing for an hour buys you jaw pain, not a jaw.
    6. Orbicularis squeeze3 × 10Firm squint, hold 5 seconds, release fully. This trains the ring muscle around the eye instead of stretching the skin over it.
    7. Under-eye drainage60sLight tapping with the ring finger from the inner corner outward, then down toward the ear. For puffiness, not lines. Almost no pressure.
    8. Cheek and lip holds3 × 10Puff both cheeks, hold 5 seconds, push the air side to side, then a hard lip press.

    One thing I left out: eye pulling

    Nothing about tugging the outer corner changes the shape or tilt of an eye. That angle is set by where the tendon attaches to bone. What repeated pulling does reach is the thinnest skin on your body, and stretching it is the opposite of what you want under there. The squeeze and the drainage above are the versions of this that actually do something.

    How this got harder

    1. Three working sets, not two. This is the real change. Two hard sets grows muscle, but the research on volume is consistent, and going to three on your main lifts is what speeds things up. Grinding harder on two sets does not substitute for it.
    2. Heavy stays heavy. First set of every compound sits at 5 to 8 reps. Isolation work moves up to 8 to 15, because that is where cables and lateral raises actually pay off.
    3. One intensity technique per exercise, last set only. Marked in red where it applies. Doing them on every set just wrecks recovery.
    4. Deload every seventh week. Cut to two sets and about 60 percent of your weights for one week. Six days of this without a break catches up with you around week eight, and the deload is what keeps you from finding out.
    5. Log everything. Weight and reps for every set. The thing that makes you bigger is beating last week's numbers, and you cannot beat what you did not write down.
    6. Eat for it. Adding volume without adding food gets you tired instead of big. Slight surplus, roughly a gram of protein per pound.

    Honest note on speed

    Muscle has a ceiling on how fast it comes, and no split beats it. What moves how you look in the near term is different: side delts for shoulder width, upper chest, neck thickness, and staying lean enough to see the work. That is why lateral raises show up four days a week here and why neck work is in the daily block. Those are the fastest visual levers you have.
